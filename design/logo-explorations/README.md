# Markover logo + favicon explorations

Eight deliberately different identity directions for Markover, a desktop tool for
reviewing Markdown as a structural tree and attaching feedback to individual
blocks. Every board includes a standalone mark, horizontal wordmark, and
favicon/app-icon treatment.

These are concept images, not final vector artwork. A selected direction should
be redrawn, optically corrected, tested at 16/32/128 px, and exported as SVG and
platform icon assets.

## 01 — Editorial redline

Bookish and sophisticated. A high-contrast `M` is crossed by a proofreader's
revision stroke and topped by a caret.

![Editorial redline](01-editorial-redline.png)

## 02 — Tree annotation

Systematic and calm. A geometric `M` grows from a central branch and ends in a
comment-like terminal node.

![Tree annotation](02-tree-annotation.png)

## 03 — Swiss panes

Rational and product-forward. Split source/review panes become a compact
blue-and-red document glyph.

![Swiss panes](03-swiss-panes.png)

## 04 — Markdown terminal

Coding-native and energetic. A custom monospaced `M` is cut by the familiar
Markdown slash in electric lime.

![Markdown terminal](04-markdown-terminal.png)

## 05 — Sticky fold

Friendly and approachable. A yellow note folds into an aubergine `M` with a
simple, high-contrast app icon.

![Sticky fold](05-sticky-fold.png)

## 06 — Brutalist stamp

Forceful and toolmaker-like. A condensed black `M` is overwritten by a vivid
review stroke.

![Brutalist stamp](06-brutalist-stamp.png)

## 07 — Continuous handoff

Humane and quietly premium. One continuous ribbon passes through an `m`, a
revision loop, and a return gesture.

![Continuous handoff](07-continuous-handoff.png)

## 08 — Overprint layers

Cultured and unconventional. Two printed layers reveal an `M` and annotation
aperture in their overlap.

![Overprint layers](08-overprint-layers.png)
