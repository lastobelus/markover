# Markover logo explorations 01–02

Built with the built-in image generation tool. Each board presents the standalone symbol, horizontal “Markover” wordmark, and favicon/app-icon treatment.

## 01 — Editorial redline

Prompt:

> Use case: logo-brand
> Asset type: clean identity presentation board for a desktop app logo and favicon
> Primary request: Design a sophisticated editorial-redline identity for Markover, a small Electron app for reviewing Markdown as a structural tree, attaching block-level feedback, and handing copyable notes to an agent.
> Scene/backdrop: one flat warm-paper presentation board, no environmental scene and no device mockups.
> Subject: A distinctive vector-friendly logo mark formed from an uppercase M integrated with a proofreader’s slash or caret. The concept should feel bookish, human, editorial, and confident without depicting a literal pen. Show exactly three coordinated applications: (1) standalone symbol, (2) horizontal wordmark combining the symbol with the exact brand name, and (3) favicon/app-icon crop of the same symbol.
> Style/medium: refined flat graphic identity system, crisp vector-like geometry, sophisticated editorial typography; confident serif/sans pairing; restrained optical details; professionally art-directed, original and memorable.
> Composition/framing: 3:2 landscape identity board, generous negative space, carefully aligned three-part layout, consistent scale logic, symbol large at left/top, horizontal wordmark clearly legible, favicon crop isolated in a simple square. No mockup clutter, no stationery, no phones, no browser chrome.
> Lighting/mood: flat print-design presentation, calm and assured.
> Color palette: warm cream paper, dark ink/near-black, one coral redline accent. Maximum three colors.
> Materials/textures: mostly flat color; only a barely perceptible paper warmth, no distressing or gradients.
> Text (verbatim): "Markover"
> Constraints: Spell Markover exactly once in the horizontal wordmark. Keep symbol, wordmark, and favicon stylistically identical and visibly related. All important forms must remain clear at 16 px. Use only the requested brand text.
> Avoid: literal pen, pencil, feather, book illustration, generic checkmark, generic letter M, logos resembling existing major brands, seals, badges, slogans, explanatory labels, design annotations, color swatches, lorem ipsum, watermarks, signatures, shadows, bevels, 3D, photorealism, mockup clutter, tiny illegible text.

Rationale: A high-contrast editorial M is “corrected” by a coral slash and caret, making review and markup immediately legible while keeping the voice warm, literary, and compact enough for an app icon.

## 02 — Tree annotation

Prompt:

> Use case: logo-brand
> Asset type: clean identity presentation board for a desktop app logo and favicon
> Primary request: Create a more compact, distinctive structural-tree plus annotation identity for Markover, a small Electron app for reviewing Markdown as a structural tree and attaching block-level feedback. The core mark must be ONE unified glyph: two angled branches form an uppercase M, while one small rectangular node and a single inward notch create the subtle tail/negative-space cue of a comment. It should feel like a Markdown outline distilled into a typographic monogram, not an org chart.
> Scene/backdrop: one flat warm-cream presentation board only.
> Subject: Show exactly three coordinated applications of the same unified glyph: (1) standalone symbol, (2) horizontal wordmark with the exact brand name, and (3) favicon/app-icon crop. The standalone mark should have no more than three node terminals total and no row of child nodes.
> Style/medium: rigorous flat vector-like identity; bold geometric strokes and carved negative space; technical, calm, minimal; contemporary humanist grotesk wordmark; optically balanced and original.
> Composition/framing: 3:2 landscape board with generous negative space; three applications arranged left-to-right with no divider lines; large standalone symbol, legible horizontal wordmark, simple square favicon crop. No device or stationery mockups.
> Lighting/mood: flat graphic presentation, systematic and quietly confident.
> Color palette: deep forest green, warm cream, dark ink; maximum three flat colors.
> Materials/textures: pure flat color, no gradients, no texture.
> Text (verbatim): "Markover"
> Constraints: Spell Markover exactly once. Use the same glyph construction in all three applications. Keep the glyph compact and legible at 16 px. The M reading should be primary, hierarchy secondary, comment cue tertiary. One unified silhouette, no disconnected diagram beneath it.
> Avoid: org chart, row of child nodes, generic connected-dots network, flowchart, Git branch logo, molecule, circuit board, literal tree, literal page, separate speech bubble, chat icon pasted on, braces, code symbols, logos resembling existing major brands, slogans, explanatory labels, design annotations, color swatches, dividers, watermarks, shadows, bevels, 3D, photorealism, mockup clutter, tiny text.

Rationale: The compact forest-green M uses branching construction and a single terminal node to suggest document hierarchy; the center notch and dangling block add a restrained annotation/comment cue without becoming a generic network diagram.
